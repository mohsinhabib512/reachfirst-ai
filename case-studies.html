A useful case-study standard

Evidence needs enough context to be meaningful.

Every future case study should help a reader understand what changed, what was done, and how the evidence should be interpreted.

  1. 01

    Starting point

    Business model, audience, market, constraints, and the original state.

  2. 02

    Defined challenge

    The problem, priority, or opportunity the engagement was meant to address.

  3. 03

    Approach

    The research, strategy, creative, campaigns, technology, and collaboration involved.

  4. 04

    Evidence & limits

    Relevant measures, comparison period, attribution boundaries, and factors that may affect transferability.

How to read results

A case study is evidence, not a forecast.

Performance varies with the market, offer, competition, starting point, website, data, budget, implementation, seasonality, measurement method, and time period.

We use published examples to show how a problem was approached. A recommendation for another organization should still begin with its own goals, systems, audience, constraints, and baseline.

Case study questions

Proof should be useful, not promotional noise.

These answers explain what is included today and how future stories should be evaluated.

Why is one case study shown in more detail?

AKRoN Roofing has a published Reach First case study describing its challenge, services, keyword outcome, and reported search changes. Other publicly listed projects do not currently provide the same level of evidence, so we do not invent it.

Do these results guarantee similar performance?

No. Every organization has a different market, baseline, offer, competition, budget, implementation, and measurement environment.

Will automation case studies be added?

They should be added when Reach First can publish an approved client story with enough context, scope, evidence, and appropriate confidentiality safeguards.

Can a client engagement remain confidential?

Yes. Publication should depend on permission and any contractual, privacy, security, or commercial restrictions. An anonymized story may be considered only when it remains accurate and approved.
