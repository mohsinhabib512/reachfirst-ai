The delivery model

Five phases. A decision at every transition.

Each phase creates something reviewable before more time or budget is committed. The exact activities and deliverables depend on the engagement.

  1. 01

    Assess

    Understand the work as it operates today.

    We examine the business objective, current steps, systems, data, people, pain points, dependencies, and exceptions before recommending a solution.

    • Stakeholder and workflow discovery
    • Systems, access, and data review
    • Opportunity and constraint mapping
    Review pointIs this the right problem to solve?Typical output: current-state map and opportunity brief
  2. 02

    Define

    Turn the opportunity into an agreed plan.

    We establish the intended outcome, baseline, scope, responsibilities, measures, risks, and what is intentionally outside the first release.

    • Success measures and acceptance criteria
    • Solution or campaign approach
    • Roadmap, roles, and decision boundaries
    Review pointIs the scope useful, feasible, and understood?Typical output: proposed approach and scoped roadmap
  3. 03

    Pilot

    Test the riskiest assumptions at a useful scale.

    We create a focused version, test normal and exception paths, gather feedback, and compare what happened with the agreed evaluation criteria.

    • Focused build or initial campaign
    • Functional, quality, and exception testing
    • User or audience feedback review
    Review pointWhat did we learn, and should we proceed?Typical output: pilot findings and recommended changes
  4. 04

    Implement

    Prepare the work for dependable use.

    We complete the agreed configuration, integrations, content, testing, documentation, approvals, training, and rollout activities.

    • Production implementation and quality assurance
    • Permissions, handoffs, and escalation paths
    • Documentation, training, and launch readiness
    Review pointAre the people, process, and system ready?Typical output: approved launch and operating guidance
  5. 05

    Improve

    Measure what matters and refine responsibly.

    After launch, agreed monitoring and support reveal performance, failures, new requirements, and the next improvements worth prioritizing.

    • Performance and issue monitoring
    • Support, maintenance, and optimization
    • Improvement backlog and change decisions
    Review pointWhat should be maintained, changed, or expanded?Typical output: performance review and prioritized next steps

Before we begin

Practical questions about the engagement.

These answers set general expectations. The proposal and project plan define the specific arrangement.

Do all projects use all five phases?

The thinking applies across engagements, but the depth and deliverables vary. A focused assessment may end with a roadmap, while a larger engagement may continue through implementation and ongoing improvement.

How do you decide what to pilot?

We look for a use case or campaign that is valuable enough to learn from, bounded enough to evaluate, and suitable for testing without unnecessary disruption.

Can you work with our existing systems and partners?

Often, subject to the available integrations, permissions, vendor constraints, security requirements, responsibilities, and the agreed scope.

When will we know the timeline and cost?

After we understand the workflow, intended outcome, systems, data, access, dependencies, and support needs. We then propose a scope, plan, assumptions, and commercial arrangement for review.

What happens if the pilot does not support the original idea?

That is useful evidence. We review why, then recommend whether to adjust the approach, address a prerequisite, test a different direction, or stop rather than scale an unsuitable solution.
