Where it can help

Useful when the same work is coordinated again and again.

The strongest candidates are repeatable enough to map, but still need clear handling for exceptions.

  • 01

    Requests arrive through several channels and need manual routing.

  • 02

    Staff re-enter the same information into multiple systems.

  • 03

    Approvals, reminders, or handoffs depend on someone remembering the next step.

  • 04

    Documents or status updates are repeatedly moved between teams.

What this can include

Automation built around the process, not a generic tool.

Scope depends on the workflow and available system connections.

01

Intake and routing

Capture requests, organize useful details, and direct work to the appropriate queue or person.

02

Tasks and approvals

Create next steps, reminders, and review gates based on agreed rules.

03

Data and document movement

Transfer approved information between connected systems while reducing repeated entry.

04

Notifications and records

Prepare status updates, staff notifications, and system records at the right stage.

Workflow questions

Before automating a process.

Does every step need to be automated?

No. The useful design may automate only repeatable steps while preserving staff judgment and existing manual controls elsewhere.

Can you connect our current tools?

Potentially. We assess APIs, permissions, data access, and project requirements before recommending a connection.

How is success evaluated?

Evaluation criteria are agreed for the scoped workflow and may include completion, routing accuracy, exception handling, adoption, or time spent.
