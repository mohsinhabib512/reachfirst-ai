A useful starting point

Planning helps separate useful, feasible opportunities from ideas that need more evidence or preparation.

  • 01

    Several teams have automation ideas, but there is no shared priority.

  • 02

What this can include

Clarity about the work, the opportunity, and the next decision.

The exact outputs depend on scope. A planning engagement can combine the following components.

01

Workflow discovery

Map current steps, people, systems, inputs, decisions, exceptions, and delays.

02

Opportunity assessment

Compare potential use cases by usefulness, feasibility, dependencies, and operational risk.

03

Solution direction

Define where rules, integrations, AI assistance, or staff review may fit.

04

Implementation roadmap

Sequence a focused pilot, required preparation, evaluation approach, and possible next stages.

Planning boundaries

Recommendations depend on what the evidence supports.

We assess what systems can expose, what information is available, where judgment matters, and who owns each operational decision.

A roadmap does not assume every process should be automated. Sometimes the useful next step is process clarification, data preparation, or a smaller pilot.

Planning questions

Before a consulting engagement.

Do we need to know which AI tools we want?

No. The engagement starts with the business process and decision, then considers appropriate technical options.

What information should we prepare?

Existing process notes, system names, representative examples, known constraints, and access to the people who understand the work are useful starting inputs.

Does planning commit us to implementation?

No. The purpose is to make the next decision clearer. Any implementation would be scoped separately.

Bring the process, not the solution

Which workflow should we examine first?

We’ll discuss the current work, the systems involved, and the decision you need the planning to support.

Helpful to bring: the process owner, current steps, systems used, and known pain points.
