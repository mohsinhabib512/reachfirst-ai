The client lifecycle

Keep context moving as the relationship changes.

A strong operating design connects the commercial promise to onboarding, delivery, review, billing, and the next client decision.

  1. 01

    Attract & qualify

    Help suitable prospects understand the expertise, fit, and next step while capturing useful context.

  2. 02

    Scope & propose

    Coordinate requirements, estimates, internal review, versions, responsibilities, and client decisions.

  3. 03

    Contract & onboard

    Move approved commitments into a structured start with documents, access, contacts, tasks, and expectations.

  4. 04

    Plan & resource

    Align the right people, skills, timing, workload, milestones, and dependencies with the agreed scope.

  5. 05

    Deliver & approve

    Keep work, discussions, files, decisions, changes, and approvals connected to the engagement.

  6. 06

    Bill, learn & grow

    Coordinate completion, billing inputs, feedback, knowledge capture, renewal, and suitable follow-up.

Example blueprint

Turn an accepted proposal into a prepared engagement.

This fictional example shows how a consulting engagement might begin. The actual design would reflect your contracts, policies, systems, review requirements, and team responsibilities.

Illustrative workflow only. It does not sign agreements, create client records, grant access, approve work, issue invoices, or promise a business result.

Sample: a client approves a project proposal
  1. 01

    Confirm the approved source

    Verify the accepted scope, named client contacts, commercial terms, and internal owner.

  2. 02

    Prepare the engagement record

    Create or update the approved workspace with relevant context, milestones, and responsibilities.

  3. 03

    Route internal preparation

    Assign kickoff tasks, access checks, required documents, and questions to the right people.

  4. 04

    Coordinate client onboarding

    Send approved information requests and schedule the kickoff after a person reviews the plan.

  5. 05

    Open the delivery path

    Make the current scope, decisions, ownership, status, and next review point visible to the team.

Common questions

Designed around your engagement model.

The right solution depends on how your firm sells, delivers, reviews, bills, and protects client work.

Which professional service firms do you support?

Examples include consulting, agencies, recruitment, legal services, accounting, advisory, IT services, architecture, engineering, training, creative studios, and other B2B service firms. The workflow matters more than fitting a strict category.

Do we need to replace our CRM or project-management platform?

Not necessarily. We first review what your current systems can support, where context is duplicated or missing, and whether configuration, integration, a focused custom layer, or replacement is appropriate.

Can you help connect sales and delivery?

Yes. A focused scope can examine the handoff from opportunity, estimate, and proposal into onboarding, resourcing, delivery, approvals, and billing inputs.

How do you handle confidential or regulated information?

Requirements differ by firm, jurisdiction, contract, and system. Discovery should identify information sensitivity, authorized access, retention, review, and where automation is inappropriate. Specialized legal, security, or compliance advice may still be required.

What should we bring to an initial discussion?

Bring one recent engagement, the path it followed, the tools and documents involved, the people responsible, and the handoff or decision point you most want to improve.
